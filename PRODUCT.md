# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Hiring managers, recruiters, and engineering peers evaluating Anton L for freelance/full-time frontend work; developers reading the blog for Vue/React/TypeScript, terminal workflow, and self-hosted/homelab content. Context is typically a quick credibility scan (projects, about) or a focused technical read (blog post).

## Product Purpose
A personal blog and projects section that establishes engineering credibility: showcase production frontend work (Vue, Nuxt, React, Next.js, TypeScript), share practical technical writing, and make it easy to get in touch for freelance/full-time opportunities. Success = a visitor trusts the skill level fast and either reads a post, browses a project, or reaches out.

## Brand Personality
Precise, technical, understated. Quiet engineering confidence — no marketing gloss, no hype copy. Voice is direct and detail-oriented (the README, about page, and "details that usually get skipped" framing all confirm this).

## Anti-references
- Generic SaaS landing page: no gradient heroes, hero-metric blocks, eyebrow kickers, or AI-template scaffolding.
- Over-designed agency projects page: no scroll-jacking, oversized type stunts, or style-over-substance flourishes that outshine the work being shown.

## Design Principles
1. Show the work, don't sell it — let projects, code, and writing carry the credibility; avoid marketing language.
2. Quiet confidence over loud design — restraint is the brand, not blandness; precision in spacing/type/color is itself the statement.
3. Content is the interface — blog posts and project entries are the product; chrome (nav, cards, badges) should recede.
4. Bilingual parity — every design decision must hold up identically in English and Russian copy lengths.
5. Fast and lightweight — static Astro site; no design choice should add meaningful JS or blocking weight.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG AA baseline (already partially in place: skip link, `:focus-visible` outlines, dark/light theme tokens). No additional stated requirements beyond AA — contrast and motion-reduction should still be verified, not assumed.
