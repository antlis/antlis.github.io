My browser is permanently polluted with YouTube tabs. Every time I see something I want to watch, I
open it in a new tab "for later" instead of doing anything that would actually save it. The tabs
pile up, and then I forget about most of them. Then I do it again.

I also never liked the YouTube page itself. Whatever I came for, there is a feed, a sidebar and a
row of suggestions competing for the same few square inches. So I did what I usually do with an
annoyance that is small enough to fix: I wrote a tool for it.

The result is **[unbloated-youtube](https://github.com/antlis/unbloated-youtube)**, a small
desktop YouTube client for Linux. It shows your subscriptions, playlists and history, plays video
in [mpv](https://mpv.io), and keeps "watch later" as an actual list instead of a graveyard of tabs.

## Where the idea came from

It started with a video. I watched [I'm Using GPUI For Everything](https://youtu.be/SdJXHYuRr48)
from Syntax. [GPUI](https://crates.io/crates/gpui) is the Rust UI framework behind the
[Zed](https://zed.dev) editor, and I had only known it as "the thing Zed is made of". Watching
someone use it for everything, not just an editor, made me curious enough to want to try it myself.

A framework you have only watched a video about is not much use, so I needed a project. The tab
problem was right there. I opened a repository, started a session with an AI coding assistant
(Claude Code), and described what I wanted: a window where I can browse YouTube, play a video, and
put things in a list instead of a tab.

The first version was a window that could play a video. The part that turns a demo into
something I actually use every day took many more sessions, and that is most of this article.

## What it is

The whole idea is to reuse what already exists instead of reimplementing it:

- **GPUI** draws the interface.
- **mpv** plays the video. It is embedded in the window, so playback feels like part of the app.
- **yt-dlp** supplies the data: lists of videos, search results, stream URLs.
- **YouTube's InnerTube API** handles actions that change your account: subscribe, like, save to a
  playlist, Watch later. It uses your browser's cookies, so there is no separate login flow
  (more on that below).

Everything slow, whether it is yt-dlp, a network request or an mpv query, runs on a background
thread, so the interface never waits. Lists are cached on disk and appear instantly the next time,
then refresh behind the scenes. The release binary is a single file of about 25 MB.

## Configure what you need, hide what you don't

"Unbloated" here means something specific: the app does not decide for you which parts of YouTube
you want to see. Nearly every element is a switch on the settings page, and there is a search
field on that page too, because there are enough switches to need one.

Do you not want recommendations under the player? Turn them off. Comments are off by default and
only load when you open their tab. Each action button (subscribe, like, dislike, share, download,
open in browser) has its own switch, and so do view counts, upload dates and subscriber counts. If
a part of the interface does not earn its place for you, it goes away.

## Watch later instead of more tabs

This is the feature the whole thing was built for.

- **A Watch later tab** next to Recommended, Comments and Up next. It is your real YouTube Watch
  later playlist, loaded when you open the tab, with a remove button on every row.
- **A Watch later button on every video, in every list.** Hover a video in your subscriptions, in
  search results, in recommendations or in history and click it. You don't have to open the video
  first, and you never open a new tab. There is also a hotkey for the video that is playing.
- **An Up next queue** for "this one next". Add anything with the `+` on its row, and autoplay
  works through the queue before it falls back to the list you picked the video from.

Two more buttons I use constantly: **Download**, which saves the video through yt-dlp, and
**Open in browser**, for the rare video that really needs the YouTube page. Both are one click or
one key, and neither changes anything else in the window.

The same list-first approach goes further. Subscriptions are a plain channel list with the
channels that have new uploads at the top and an unseen count next to each. You can sort channels
into your own groups (Music, Tech) and filter the feed by group. Groups live only in the app, not
on YouTube. History remembers where you stopped in every video and offers "Continue watching".

## Built for the keyboard

Much of my day is spent in a terminal, so the app had to work without a mouse. Playback has the keys you
would expect (space, arrows, `j` and `l` for ten seconds, `f` for fullscreen, `m` to mute), `?`
lists all of them, and the number keys switch tabs.

There is also an optional **Vim mode**. It adds `j`/`k` movement in lists and a hint mode in the
style of [Vimium](https://github.com/philc/vimium): press `f` and every clickable thing gets a
short label. Type the label to click it.

## Accessibility: a visible focus ring

Hint mode is for people who like it. Focus handling is for everyone. `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` move a
visible ring through every button, row and switch, and `Enter` or `Space` presses whatever has
the ring. `Esc` clears it.

ring you can always see is the cheapest way to make sure a keyboard user is never lost.

## Building it with an AI assistant

I want to be straight about how this was made. Most of the Rust was written by the assistant. My
part was deciding what the app should do, trying it in the real app and saying what was wrong.
That workflow has some specific consequences:

- **There are no automated tests.** The assistant checks UI changes by running the app on a private
  X display, driving it with `xdotool` and taking screenshots, then looking at the screenshots.
  That is slower than unit tests and catches things unit tests never would, such as a menu hanging
  off the bottom of the window.
- **My bug reports were screenshots.** Most of the fixes started with me pasting a picture and
  saying "this is wrong".
- **I had to keep deciding what the app is.** An assistant will happily add a feature for every
  idea. The settings page exists because I kept asking for things to be optional.

A few problems were instructive:

- **The video is not my window.** mpv draws into its own native X11 window, placed over the video
  area. GPUI cannot draw on top of it, so any menu or tooltip that reaches into the video is
  hidden behind it. I kept finding new ones. Menus are now kept inside the left column, and the
  save-to-playlist overlay hides the video while it is open, but tooltips over the video are still
  a known limit.
- **My window was bigger than my screen.** It opened at a fixed size, and with display scaling that
  could be taller than the screen, which pushed the bottom of the right column (including a "Load
  more comments" button) off the edge. It now opens no bigger than the screen.
- **Comments are awkward.** yt-dlp cannot stream comments, so "Load more" refetches from the top
  with a higher limit, and every click is slower than the last. A proper fix means talking to
  InnerTube directly.
- **Compile time.** The dependency tree has about 730 crates, mostly from GPUI. A clean Nix build
  took 26 minutes on my machine. Nobody should have to wait for that, so a GitHub Actions workflow
  builds the binary in about seven minutes whenever I push a tag.

## Installing it

You need `mpv`, `yt-dlp` and `deno` (yt-dlp uses it to solve YouTube's JavaScript challenges).
Then pick one:

```bash title="Arch / AUR"
yay -S unbloated-youtube-bin
```

Or download the Linux x86_64 archive from the
[releases page](https://github.com/antlis/unbloated-youtube/releases) and run the binary. A Nix
package is in the repository too. Releases are cut by a tag: the workflow builds the binary,
publishes the release and updates the AUR package, so the AUR copy tracks GitHub without me
touching it.

Subscriptions, playlists, history and the account buttons need a login. For now that means
pointing the app at a browser that is already logged in to YouTube:

```toml title="~/.config/unbloated-youtube/config.toml"
cookies_from_browser = "brave+gnomekeyring"   # yt-dlp syntax: "firefox", "chromium", ...
```

Without it, search and playback still work.

## Where it stands

It is experimental. It only runs on Linux with X11 (Wayland is untested), the login is clumsy, and
there are rough edges I have not found yet. I am still satisfied with it, which is the point: it
already does the one job I built it for. I am going to stop watching YouTube in the browser. No
more tabs I will never read, and no more feed fighting for my attention. I open the app, pick
something, and the rest goes into Watch later.

## What's next

None of this is built yet. It is the list I am working from:

- **A description tab** under the player, next to Comments and Chapters.
- **Better comments**: replies, sorting by top or newest, clickable timestamps that seek the video,
  loading more without refetching, and maybe posting.
- **Casting.** Chromecast-style "play this on the TV" is the obvious idea. Closer to home, I already
  run a homelab box connected to my TV with [tg-mpv-bot](/blog/tg-mpv-bot), and sending a video to
  that mpv is the version I want most.
- **Import and export.** Groups, settings and per-channel switches live in one folder on one
  machine today, with no sync and no backup.
- **A better login.** Reading cookies from a browser works but is fragile. I am looking at a
  guided setup in the settings page and, later, an in-app login window.
- **More ways to install it**: `.deb` and `.rpm` packages, an AppImage, a Flatpak, crates.io, and
  maybe bundling yt-dlp and deno so there is less to install.
- **Wayland, then Windows and macOS.** The embedded player is the main thing that ties it to X11,
  though not the only Linux-specific piece.
- **Smaller things**: a startup check that tells you which of mpv, yt-dlp or deno is missing, and
  removing entries from YouTube's own history, not just the app's copy.

If any of that sounds useful, or you try it and something breaks,
[open an issue](https://github.com/antlis/unbloated-youtube/issues). The README lists every
feature and shortcut.
